# Schwung — Physiotherapist Review Packet (draft for review)

**For:** a physiotherapist (or equivalently qualified exercise professional).
**Status:** all four habits are **HELD** and unavailable in the app. **There is no clinical sign-off.** Every
exercise, target and stop rule below is a **product draft** for you to approve, change or reject.

**About the app.** Schwung is a free, offline habit app for people aged 16+. It runs a 66-day program in three
phases: Days 1–21, 22–44 and 45–66.
- **Daily list:** people tick off a short list of habits each day. Each habit has three levels (Easy, Medium,
  Hard).
- **Early level-up:** after 7 completions in a row, the person is *offered* the next level and can accept or
  decline.
- **Step-down:** after 3 missed sessions in a row, the habit steps down one level, never below Easy.
- **Strength and balance never level up automatically** at Day 22 or Day 45 (owner decision). They move up only
  when the person accepts an offer.
- **Scheduling:** habits can be scheduled on 2–3 chosen weekdays; rest days never count as misses.
- **No supervision:** no video, no assessment, no clinician contact.

**Evidence used (search-level only; full texts not read).** Sources are in `SCHWUNG_V2_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md`.
- WHO 2020 and UK CMO 2019 recommend strength on 2+ days a week, and balance-and-strength activity on 3+ days a
  week for older adults.
- Cochrane (Sherrington 2019): balance and functional exercise lowers the fall rate by about 24%, with larger
  effects when delivered by a health professional.
- Cochrane (Liu & Latham): progressive resistance training gives large strength gains; adverse events are poorly
  reported.
- Chair-based exercise (Klempel 2021) improves strength measures.
- NHS sitting and strength-and-balance sheets.

None of this validates the specific reps, holds or steps below.

**Questions that apply to all four habits**
- **A.** Is an unsupervised app habit appropriate for this group at all, or only alongside a referral line
  ("Ask your GP about a local strength and balance class")?
- **B.** Do the stop conditions cover what you'd require? What must the app say before the first session?
- **C.** Is offering the next level after 7 completions in a row safe, or should progression require something
  else (e.g. "felt easy" self-report)?
- **D.** Is a step-down after 3 missed sessions appropriate after a break?

## 1. Beginner Strength Preparation (`move_strength_prep`)
**Intended users:** adults and 16–17-year-olds who are inactive or lightly active (0–2 active days a week), with
no precautions. Offered by choice only, never automatically.

**Screening and exclusion (app answers):**
- Excluded: heart or blood-pressure condition; pregnancy or recently gave birth; walking or standing for long is
  difficult; already active 3+ days a week.
- Routed to the Gentle variant instead (#2): 65+, joint, back or injury pain, or skipped health questions.

**Proposed exercises** (from NHS strength sheets):
1. **Wall push-up:** hands on a wall at shoulder height, step back, bend and straighten the arms.
2. **Sit-to-stand:** from a firm chair without arms if possible; hands across the chest or on the thighs.
3. **Calf raise:** holding a worktop or chair back, rise onto the toes and lower slowly.

**Proposed targets (product draft):**

| Level | Target |
|---|---|
| Easy | 1 move × 5–8 reps |
| Medium | 2 moves × 8 reps |
| Hard | 3 moves × 8–10 reps |

Always stop 2–3 reps before it gets hard.

**Frequency and recovery:** scheduled **2 days a week** (owner decision), with an optional third day pending
your review. Rest days never count as misses.

**Progression:** an offer after 7 completed sessions in a row (about 3.5 weeks at 2 days a week). Never at Day
22 or 45. A step-down after 3 missed sessions.

**Stop conditions (copy draft):** "Breathe normally; don't hold your breath. Stop if you feel pain, dizziness,
chest discomfort or unusual breathlessness. On a sore day, rest."

**Questions for you:**
1. Are these three moves suitable unsupervised for beginners aged 16+? Any to replace?
2. Are the rep ranges right? Should the reps be "until it feels moderately hard (about 5–6 out of 10)" instead
   of fixed numbers?
3. 2 days a week: is a third day reasonable for this dose? Minimum rest between sessions?
4. Teens (16–17): any change in the moves or wording?
5. Is "Breathe normally" enough for blood-pressure safety, given heart/BP is already excluded?

## 2. Gentle Strength Preparation (variant for older adults and joint pain)
**Intended users:** 65+, or any adult with joint, back or injury pain, or who skipped the health questions.
Choice only.

**Screening and exclusion:**
- Excluded: heart or blood-pressure condition, pregnancy, walking or standing difficulty.
- **Proposed new screen:** "Can you get up from a chair and stand for about a minute without help from another
  person?" Yes is required. *A new quiz item needs owner approval.*

**Proposed exercises:**
1. **Sit-to-stand** from a firm chair, using the armrests if needed.
2. **Wall push-up** with the feet close to the wall.
3. **Heel raises**, holding a worktop with both hands.

**Proposed targets (product draft):**

| Level | Target |
|---|---|
| Easy | 1 move × 5 slow reps, holding a support |
| Medium | 2 moves × 5–8 reps |
| Hard | 3 moves × 8–10 reps |

**Frequency and recovery:** 2 days a week (scheduled), or daily practice at this low dose: **your call**.

**Progression:** acceptance only (7 sessions in a row); never automatic.

**Stop conditions:** "Move slowly and keep a support within reach. Stop if anything hurts sharply, or you feel
dizzy, faint, unusually breathless or have chest pain."

**Questions for you:**
1. Are these moves and supports safe unsupervised for 65+ and for people with joint pain?
2. For joint pain: should the copy say "stop if it hurts", or NICE's "some discomfort may rise at first; stop if
   pain is sharp or lasts into the next day"?
3. Daily or 2 days a week at this dose?
4. Should people with osteoporosis or a fracture history be excluded (a new question)?
5. Is the chair-stand screen enough, or is a falls question also required?

## 3. Supported Balance Practice (`move_balance`)
**Intended users:** 65+ (or 50+ by choice) with a fitness or calm goal **and** Yes to the chair-stand screen.
Choice only at first.

**Exclusion:**
- No or Not sure on the screen; walking or standing difficulty; skipped health questions.
- Proposed new options: a fall in the last year, dizziness or vertigo, recent surgery or fracture.

**Set-up (shown every time):** stand at a kitchen worktop or the back of a sturdy chair that won't move, with a
clear floor around you. Supportive shoes, or barefoot (no socks on smooth floors). Have someone nearby if you're
unsure.

**Proposed exercise:** a supported single-leg stand. Stand tall, lift one foot slightly and hold without leaning
on the support more than needed, then swap legs. Based on NHS-trust balance sheets, which describe holds of up to
30 s; ours stops at 15 s by design.

**Proposed targets (product draft):**

| Level | Target |
|---|---|
| Easy | 3 holds × 5 s each leg, both hands on the support |
| Medium | 3 × 10 s, one hand |
| Hard | 3 × 15 s, fingertips |

**Frequency:** daily or scheduled: **your call** (owner decision 2). WHO's guideline is 3+ days a week of
balance and strength.

**Progression:** acceptance only. Support is never reduced by the calendar.

**Stop conditions:** "Stop and sit down if you feel dizzy, faint or unsteady beyond what your support can handle,
or have chest pain or new pain. Tell a professional if this happens."

**Questions for you:**
1. Is the supported single-leg stand the right first exercise, or should it be tandem or semi-tandem stance?
2. Are the hold times and the support steps (two hands → one hand → fingertips) safe self-progressions?
3. Daily, or 3 days a week?
4. Is the chair-stand screen sufficient? Which of the proposed exclusions are required?
5. Should balance only be offered with a referral to a strength-and-balance class? Supervised programmes (e.g.
   Otago) show the larger effects.

## 4. Seated Mobility (`move_seated`; not in the app yet)
**Intended users:** people who find walking or standing for long difficult (an existing quiz answer). Choice
only, never automatic.

**Exclusion (needs individual advice):**
- recent surgery, fracture or joint replacement
- severe or unexplained pain
- neurological conditions affecting movement or balance
- osteoporosis with a fracture history (twisting moves)
- neck problems (neck move); heart conditions with exercise restrictions
- pressure-injury risk for wheelchair users
- anyone told by a clinician to limit activity

**Proposed exercises** (NHS sitting exercises), seated upright in a sturdy chair without wheels, feet flat:
1. **Chest stretch:** hold 5–10 s.
2. **Upper-body twist:** arms crossed, turn the upper body; hold 5 s each side.
3. **Hip march:** lift one knee as far as comfortable; alternate.
4. **Arm raises:** breathe out going up.
5. **Ankle stretch:** straighten a leg; toes towards you, then away.
6. **Neck rotation:** hold 5 s each side; only without neck problems.

**Proposed targets (product draft):**

| Level | Target |
|---|---|
| Easy | 2 moves of your choice, 5 times each |
| Medium | 4 moves, 5 times each |
| Hard | all moves you can do comfortably, 5 times each |

**Frequency:** daily (low load). The NHS page suggests at least twice a week.

**Progression:** acceptance only (proposed).

**Stop conditions:** "Stop if anything hurts, or you feel dizzy or short of breath. Move only as far as is
comfortable."

**Questions for you:**
1. Which moves are unsuitable for wheelchair users, or with osteoporosis (twisting)?
2. Should a seated strength move be added (e.g. sit-to-stand from a high chair with arms, only for those who can
   already stand unaided)?
3. Daily, or 2–3 days a week?
4. Is "move only as far as is comfortable" enough guidance for range of motion?
